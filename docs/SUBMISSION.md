# Challenge submission notes

**Challenge:** NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer
**Project:** Luna Crew: Moon Base Mission
**Team:** Maja Madi
**Languages:** English, Japanese

## High-Level Summary

Kids plan a small Moon base, one work-point choice at a time. The jobs do not weigh the same: Power is strong in the day and weak at night, Life Support makes air and water but spends power, Shield matters more when a saved NASA storm arrives, and Science earns learning points without filling the safety bars. If a bar hits zero, the crew goes home safe.

## Detailed Description

The game is a static page. English and Japanese. A mission map unlocks eight lessons, from a one-day tutorial to the full three-day mission, a daily storm seed, and a harder challenge. Stars, ranks and a science journal stay in the browser. Cutscenes can be skipped. Reduced motion shows still frames. Every shown fact has a checked https source. Storms are labeled updated, saved, or training. This build never says the data is live. Sound effects and music are made in the browser with WebAudio (no audio files), and read-aloud uses the device's built-in speech voice.

## NASA Data

- DONKI flares and particle events in `data/latest.json` and `data/storms.json` drive the storm card, including the date and class when the event is real.
- OSDR study OSD-476 (plants in Apollo lunar soil) is saved in `data/osdr.json` and supports the greenhouse fact.
- RadLab: one ISS dose-rate reading is saved in `data/radlab.json` and can appear on the storm card when the file is usable.

## Space Agency Partner Data

The Canadian Space Agency food-production toolkit is a checked partner page behind the classroom food fact. It is marked Partner on the Real NASA Data screen.

## One-sentence pitch

Kids plan a Moon base for three lunar days, meet a solar storm built from saved NASA data, and see why Earth is the best base we will ever have.

## Judging criteria

### Influence

A class can play one lesson, see a real storm date, and talk about why Earth already gives us air and water. English and Japanese, a teachers page, and a demo link make that easy to start.

### Creativity

Commander Nova, Dr. Sprout and Pip are original drawings. The base, the mission path and the cutscenes are SVG. Story choices stay separate from sourced science.

### Validity

DONKI storms, OSDR study OSD-476 and a RadLab reading are saved in the repo. Labels say updated, saved or training. Shown facts need a checked https source. The meters are classroom units.

### Relevance

This is a junior astronaut mission trainer about living away from Earth: life support, power across the lunar night, and space-weather choices.

### Presentation

The page works on a phone and a laptop. Buttons are large. Reduced motion shows still frames. Cutscenes can be skipped.

### How the jobs are weighted

The jobs are not equal. Power is 5 per point in daytime and 1 at night (half of that, rounded down, if panels are damaged). Life Support is 4 per point and costs 1 Power for every 2 points. Shield is 4 per point, and storm level N needs N times 20 Shield to keep working. Science is 10 Mission Points and does not fill a safety bar. The ? button, the briefing line and the teachers table read these same numbers.
